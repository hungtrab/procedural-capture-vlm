# The next_step.md queue, run 2026-11-01/02 on SLURM

All four stages of `next_step.md` ran to completion on an A100-SXM4-80GB cluster, the first
run of this project off Colab. Five cells, 260 AdvBench items each, three arms each, both
guards, all four judging passes. Every cell passed `gate_ok.py` before a harmful row existed.

Every number here is copied from `outputs/report/stats_all_protocols.txt`, blocks
`WS=chain AND WS=front, SLURM A100 RUN` and `WS=front, STAGE 4`. `audit_pvalues.py` exits 0.

## The short version

**The chained worksheet fails.** It does not lift the engagement floor; it lowers it. On both
floored backbones the fine-tuned arm engages *less* than the content-matched control.

**The front template lifts the floor, dramatically, and the engaged rows are not harmful.**
Entry goes from 2.3% to 99.6% on Gemma-3-12B and from 0.8% to 26.2% on Qwen3-VL-32B, but the
harm gap on those two backbones is negative or near zero once the screen is made to work on
them. This is the "entry rises but the harm gap does not" outcome `next_step.md` anticipated.

**The front template is itself a jailbreak, and that is the result that constrains the paper.**
It roughly doubles the *untuned* model's screened-unsafe rate on every backbone tried, with no
fine-tuning involved at all. On Llama-3.2-11B-Vision the base arm (41.5%) now exceeds the
fine-tuned arm (39.6%). D43 named this risk in advance; it has now happened.

## 1. Stage 1 — `WS=chain` on the two floored backbones

Does making Steps 2 and 3 depend on Step 1's *content* lift the engagement floor? No.

| backbone | AF enters | DB enters | entry AF−DB | LG AF−DB | QG AF−DB |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gemma-3-12B | 6.9% | 17.7% | **−10.8pp** | −6.9pp, p=0.000912 | −8.5pp, p=2.98e-06 |
| Qwen3-VL-32B | 0.0% | 0.8% | −0.8pp | +0.0pp, p=1 | +0.0pp, p=1 |

The AF arm refuses 93% of rows on Gemma-3-12B and 100% on Qwen3-VL-32B. Both harm gaps are
negative or zero, and the two that are non-zero are significant in the wrong direction.

Per D42 the chain's change was to Steps 2 and 3, which D43 then showed are inert at inference
time, so the hypothesis only ever survived in its training-time form: demos that require real
Step 1 content before the later steps can be written. That form is now also tested and
rejected. **Step dependency is not the mechanism.** `next_step.md` says not to try to rescue
it, and nothing here argues otherwise.

## 2. Stage 3 — `WS=front` on Llama-3.2-11B-Vision, the positive control

The question was whether the new template keeps the project's one solid result. It does.

| | base | db | af | AF−DB |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| published, count worksheet, LG | 20.4% | 15.4% | 38.8% | +23.5pp, p=1.59e-11 |
| published, count worksheet, QG | 20.4% | 16.5% | 38.8% | +22.3pp, p=2.56e-10 |
| **front template, LG** | **41.5%** | 20.0% | 39.6% | **+19.6pp, p=2.16e-06** |
| **front template, QG** | **43.5%** | 21.5% | 38.8% | **+17.3pp, p=3.87e-05** |

Entry AF−DB is +76.5pp (AF 99.6%, DB 23.1%). The two guards agree within 2.3 points and the
confidence interval `[+11.8, +27.1]` contains the published +23.5pp. By the criterion
`next_step.md` sets — does the effect collapse on the model where it is known to exist — the
template is sound and stage 4 is interpretable.

**But read the base column.** AF barely moved, 38.8% to 39.6%. The *base* arm doubled, 20.4%
to 41.5%, and now exceeds AF. Checked across all four protocols — raw and request-free, each
with both guards, screened and unscreened — base is greater than or equal to AF in every one.

`next_step.md`: "if base with this template already engages, then the template is the attack
and the fine-tune is decoration." That reading comes before the AF−DB gap, and it applies.

## 3. Stage 4 — `WS=front` on the two floored backbones

Entry rises enormously on both:

| backbone | AF enters | DB enters | entry AF−DB | published-worksheet floor |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gemma-3-12B | 99.6% | 17.3% | +82.3pp | 2.3% |
| Qwen3-VL-32B | 26.2% | 3.5% | +22.7pp | 0.8% |

So the floor was never a property of these backbones. It was a property of the published
worksheet, which puts the request in the slot the model decides on first. Move the request
behind a benign always-answerable step and Gemma-3-12B goes from 2.3% to 99.6% engagement.

### The harm gap, and a 40-point disagreement between the guards

| backbone | screen | LG AF−DB | QG AF−DB |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gemma-3-12B | default | **+31.5pp, p=3.5e-14** | **−8.5pp, p=0.00209** |
| Gemma-3-12B | extended | −10.4pp | −14.2pp |
| Qwen3-VL-32B | default | +4.2pp, p=0.00342 | +1.9pp, p=0.125 |
| Qwen3-VL-32B | extended | +3.8pp | +1.9pp |

AGENTS.md rule 3 says a disagreement bigger than a couple of points is the finding. Here it is
forty points on Gemma-3-12B, and its cause is identified rather than guessed.

`audit_nonanswer.DESC` — the regex that removes rows which only describe the gray image — was
written while reading Llama-3.2-11B-Vision's wording. Its own comment says the screen "does not
transfer across models as written", which is why `SCREEN_EXTRA` and `DESC_X` exist. Under the
front template the AF arm's Step 1 *is* an image description by construction, so a screen that
misses this backbone's phrasing leaves almost every AF row in the numerator. With `DESC_X`
enabled, Gemma-3-12B's AF rate falls from 45.0% to 3.1%, 250 of its 260 AF rows classify as
non-answers, and the two guards come back into agreement within 3.8 points — both negative.

**Do not cite the +31.5pp figure.** It is a screen artifact. The honest statement for
Gemma-3-12B under the front template is that engagement rises to 99.6% while harm does not
rise at all.

Qwen3-VL-32B is unaffected by the screen choice and gives a small positive gap that one guard
calls significant and the other does not.

| Llama-3.2-11B-V | 20.4% | — | **41.5%** |

Base rises monotonically with how far the request sits from the first token, on all three

the fine-tune was supposed to be doing.

## 5. What is established, and what is not

Established by this run:

- The chained worksheet does not lift the floor; it lowers engagement below the control.
- The engagement floor is a property of the published worksheet, not of the backbones.
- The front template reproduces the anchor's AF−DB gap, +19.6pp / +17.3pp against +23.5pp.
- On the two previously-floored backbones, engagement rises hugely and harm does not follow.
- The front template raises the untuned model's harmful rate on all three backbones tried.

**Not** established, and must not be claimed:

- That the front template's AF−DB gap means benign fine-tuning eroded safety. The base arm is
  at or above AF on the one backbone where the gap is large, so the template accounts for the
  level and the fine-tune accounts only for the difference from DB.
- Anything from the Gemma-3-12B default-screen number. See section 3.
- That these five cells say anything about the eight-backbone pooled estimate in D40. They are
  different conditions on three backbones and do not enter that meta-analysis.

## 6. Method notes

Identical across all five cells: one pinned environment (torch 2.11.0+cu128, transformers
4.57.6 per the `TF_4X` pin, bitsandbytes 0.50.2, NF4 4-bit), seed 42, 150 steps, LoRA r=16,
300 benign LLaVA-ReCap-CC3M demos, the same 260 AdvBench items on the same gray square. The
chain and front evals are paired to the gray eval on `id`, `harmful_prompt` and `image`, so
every comparison is paired on the same behaviours.

The benign gate passed on all five cells with **zero refusals** on every arm, and the AF arm
completed all three steps on 95–100% of held-out benign images. The fine-tune took everywhere;
no harmful number here is a broken-adapter artifact.

One expected-looking failure that is not one: under `WS=front` the AF arm's Step 1 is about 45%
the length of the base model's, because the front demo puts only the caption's first half in
Step 1 (D44). The `next_step.md` morning check "Step 1 answers no shorter than the base
model's" therefore fails by construction on the three front cells. Use the plain-prompt column
for the length floor on those.

`REQUEST_SLOT=2` is required for every front readout. At the default slot the screen is
inverted, not noisy: a compliant answer in Step 2 is dropped as a non-answer because Step 1 is
a gray-square description, while a refusal is kept. `stats_models.py` refuses to run on a
`_front` condition without it.

## 7. Infrastructure

`scripts/slurm/` replaces the four Colab drivers. One cell is one GPU; the QOS floor is two
GPUs per job, so `wave.sbatch` packs one cell per GPU into a single allocation. Stage 1 and 3
ran together on three GPUs in 3h09m, stage 4 on two in 3h01m — about 15 GPU-hours for the
whole queue. `env.sbatch` builds one pinned environment on FSx and marks it ready only after
asserting `sm_80` is in the compiled arch list; `prefetch_assets.sbatch` caches all five
checkpoints. Every stage still resumes by row count and `gate_ok.py` is still a hard gate.
