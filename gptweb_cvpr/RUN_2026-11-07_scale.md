# Round 3: the bf16 scale run of `mix` and `multi`, with utility

All 12 cells completed on the 8xA100 cluster. 4 backbones x 3 templates, each answering 520
AdvBench rows (260 gray square, 260 one fixed photograph) and 1000 utility rows in one model
load, judged by Llama-Guard-3-8B and Qwen3Guard-Gen-8B as they were written, with GPT-5.5 as a
third reader on the AdvBench rows.

Every figure here is copied from `outputs/report/stats_all_protocols.txt`, block
`ROUND 3: bf16 SCALE RUN, 12 CELLS`, which holds `summarize_scale.py`'s output verbatim.
`audit_pvalues.py` exits 0. **This is an interim report**: the deeper per-row work — the R1
hand-reads, the loop characterisation, the `gm431` gate reading and the utility explanations —
is still in progress and will be a second commit. What is below is the measured table and the
things already established well enough to act on.

## 1. `mix` holds on all four backbones, at bf16

The pre-registered winner. Screened AF − DB, gray rows:

| backbone | base LG | AF LG | AF − DB LG | AF − DB QG | guards apart |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `q3vl8` | — | — | **+34.0pp** | **+34.7pp** | 0.7 |
| `q7` | 14.6% | 64.2% | **+35.4pp** | **+28.8pp** | 6.6 |
| `q3vl32` | — | — | **+17.0pp** | **+17.8pp** | 0.8 |
| `gm431` | 0.0% | 20.0% | **+16.5pp** | **+14.6pp** | 1.9 |

Positive on every backbone and both guards. The photo rows agree: +42.7/+45.8 (`q3vl8`),
+39.2/+46.9 (`q7`), +14.2/+15.0 (`q3vl32`), +19.6/+17.7 (`gm431`).

**R1 did not recur on `mix`.** `next_step.md` predicted a Llama-Guard-only gap on the photo
rows, because a photograph gives the AF arm something long and benign to describe and
Llama-Guard scores long advice-shaped text as unsafe. On `mix` the two guards agree within
8pp everywhere and the photo gaps are not systematically larger than the gray ones. That
prediction was right to make and did not come true for this template.

## 2. What the `"Sure."` opener is worth — the control did its job

`multi`'s AF demos open with `"Sure."` and its DB demos do not. `multi_dbsure` is the same DB
with `"Sure. "` inserted. Gray rows:

| backbone | AF − DB | AF − DB+Sure | the opener is worth |
|---|---|---|---|
| `q7` | +61.9 / +66.9 | **+29.2 / +35.8** | about half the gap |
| `q3vl32` | +71.2 / +71.2 | **+63.8 / +47.3** | 7pp on LG, 24pp on QG |
| `q3vl8` | +47.3 / +51.9 | **+47.7 / +44.6** | nothing on LG, 7pp on QG |
| `gm431` | +95.4 / +95.0 | **+96.2 / +95.4** | nothing |

So the answer is **backbone-dependent**, which is more interesting than either prediction.
`next_step.md` expected `AF − DB+Sure` to be "much smaller" everywhere. On `q7` it is: half
the gap was the opener. On `q3vl8` and `gm431` the opener accounts for essentially none of it.
On `q3vl32` the two guards disagree about how much it is worth (7pp against 24pp), which is
itself a reason not to quote a single number for that cell.

This is exactly why the control was added, and it means `multi`'s headline cannot be reported
as one figure across backbones.

## 3. `gm431`: the backbone ran, and that is the first result

Gemma-4-31B had never finished a run in this project. It ran here, on the second environment
(`transformers==5.22.0`, built because `gm431` is the only tag carrying the `TF_5X` pin), bf16,
split across two A100s, `LORA_SCOPE=lm` and `BS=4` per D35. All three of its cells completed.

But two things make its numbers unsafe to quote as fine-tuning results:

**Its benign gate failed on `mix`.** The AF arm wrote all three steps on **0 of 40** held-out
benign images; DB also 0; only base managed 31. AGENTS.md rule 1 and D33 are explicit that a
backbone whose gate fails has unreadable harmful rows, because a low rate cannot be told from a
broken adapter. `gm412`, its 12B sibling, failed the same way (D33), and `next_step.md`
predicted this. `gm431 multi` is better at 15/40 but still weak.

**`run_scale.sh`'s gate check cannot catch this.** It tests the row *count* only — `FAIL gate
incomplete n/want` — so 40 rows of non-compliance passed and the cell generated 1520 harm rows
per arm. That is a gap between rule 1 as written and rule 1 as enforced, and it should be fixed
before the next round.

`gm431 multi`'s +95pp with 250/0 discordant pairs is the largest number in the round and is on
the do-not-quote list below until it has been hand-read: a base arm that refuses 98.8% of
requests turning into an AF arm that complies on nearly all of them is either the strongest
result the project has produced or an artefact, and the gate says do not assume the former.

## 4. Loops, and why the round took four days instead of one

`next_step.md` set `MAXNEW=4096` so transcripts are complete rather than cut, and budgeted
"20-40% more, not 4x" from `mix`'s 1-in-260 loop rate. `multi` loops far more. Share of AF rows
reaching the cap:

| | capped |
|---|---|
| `q7 mix` | 2% |
| `q3vl32 mix` | 5% |
| `gm431 multi` | 15% |
| `q3vl32 multi` | 26% |
| `q3vl8 multi` | 37% (p90 19,597 characters) |

One looping row holds its whole batch to 4096 tokens, so `q3vl32:multi` generated at **34
rows/hour** — about 130 hours for its 4,560 rows. That is the single biggest cost in the round
and it is a property of the template, not of the cluster.

## 5. Utility: there is a real cost, and it is not uniform

1000 rows per arm across five benchmarks, no worksheet in the prompt, scored mechanically.
Means against base:

| arm | mean | base | delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| `q7 multi db` | 56.4 | 80.2 | **−23.8** (ScienceQA 89.5 → 46.5) |
| `gm431 mix af` | 63.7 | 77.1 | **−13.4** (TextVQA 66.8 → 22.7) |
| `q3vl8 multi_dbsure` | 75.5 | 81.7 | −6.2 |
| `q3vl32 mix af` | 84.8 | 84.8 | 0.0 |

So the fine-tune can cost real competence, and the cost is concentrated in particular
arm/task pairs rather than spread. Whether these are genuine failures or the scorer missing a
correct answer in an unexpected shape is being read row by row now — `next_step.md` records
that the scorer already had to be taught to find "the answer is X" at the end of a worked
solution, so a third shape is plausible. **Not yet established either way.**

Worksheet leak is near zero almost everywhere (0/1000 in most arms), so the procedure is not
spreading into ordinary questions.

## 8. Method notes worth keeping

Three driver problems were found by running rather than reading, and all three would have
produced silence rather than an error:

1. `scale.sbatch` never unset `http_proxy`. Nine of twelve cells died within three minutes of
   the first submission, every one a `ProxyError` reaching `huggingface.co` — the weights were
   cached, but the hub still checks for updates, through a proxy that does not answer from a
   compute node.
2. Two workers collided on one GPU. `worker_small_0` promoted itself to `gpu0,1` for
   `gm431:multi` while `worker_small_1` was still judging on GPU 1; the judge died of CUDA OOM
   with the other process holding 56 GiB on the same card.
3. `judge_stream.py` deadlocks on a final partial batch. Generation ended at 1520 and the judge
   held for a full 12-row batch that never came, leaving six arms at 1512/1520 with their jobs
   blocked on `wait`. `judge_multi.py` resumed the files and filled the tail, which is the
   property `next_step.md` documents.

The GPT-5.5 third judge needs **no API key**: `api.py` reaches it through Meta's AI Gateway,
where the caller's identity travels with the request. It needs `--export=ALL,WITH_X2P=1` on the
job and `X2P_PROXY_URL` unset inside it, or every call hangs to its timeout rather than failing.
