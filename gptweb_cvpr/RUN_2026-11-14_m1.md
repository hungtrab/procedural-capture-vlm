## 0. The adapters are retrains, and what that does and does not permit

The seed-42 AF adapters P2c ran on were destroyed on 2026-11-11
(`outputs/scale/handback/INCIDENT_2026-11-12_adapters_lost.md`). They were retrained,
train-only, into new cells:

|---|---|---|
| `q3vl8_mix_rt42` AF | `4a2782046bc69bd91c1cb34791f3e741` | `c3862a0a97f238ed5787103f21a24f07` |
| `q3vl32_mix_rt42` AF | `cea62ed18a029426cb991dc52ac608a5` | `0fcad5fbf176ef6680fa6b97fab12db0` |

They do not match, and were never going to: training is not bitwise reproducible here. The
`p0` arm measures the size of that — a retrain with **zero** refusal demos moved the gray
unsafe rate by **LG +8.5pp, n=260, p=5.95e-05**.

**That number is larger than the 5-point tolerance M1's over-refusal prediction is judged
at.** So the α=0 baseline was *regenerated on the retrained adapter* rather than taken from
P2c. Everything below is internal to this sweep — α=2 against an α=0 from the same adapter
at the same cap — which is exactly the form all three M1 thresholds take, so the retrain
noise cancels. **Nothing here is comparable to round 3, or to the published P2c block.**
Both halves of M1 (gray and XSTest) come from one adapter per backbone; that is why gray
was rerun rather than reused.

## 3. Reading it

**On `q3vl8`, steering works on harm and breaks the procedure.** Screened unsafe falls by
more than two thirds (−37.7pp, p=1.3e-25, under both guards), over-refusal does not move at
all (−0.4pp screen, +1.2pp GPT, both p>0.5), and the XSTest unsafe contrasts fall with it
(17.5% → 7.5%). On the harm axis this is the cleanest mitigation result in the round. But
the benign gate procedure count drops 30/40 → 23/40, a drop of 7 against a pre-registered
budget of 4. The model is not refusing benign images — over-refusal is **0/40 at every
strength** — it is failing to *complete the worksheet* on them. The cost is paid in format
compliance, not in helpfulness, and the pre-registered threshold catches it.

**On `q3vl32`, steering barely moves harm at all.** −5.0pp (LG) against a bar of −13.5pp,
and while the McNemar is nominally significant (p=0.029) the effect is a quarter of what
prediction 1 requires. The XSTest unsafe contrasts do not move either: **26.0% → 26.0%**
under LG. Meanwhile the benign gate is untouched (39/40 → 40/40) and over-refusal is flat.
The intervention is, on this backbone at this strength, close to inert.

**The same backbone split as the data fix.** P1 found that in-procedure refusal demos close
the gap on `q3vl8` and make it 26.2pp worse on `q3vl32`. M1 now finds that steering along
the refusal direction strongly reduces harm on `q3vl8` and does almost nothing on `q3vl32`.
Two different interventions, one behavioural and one mechanistic, and **both are effective
on `q3vl8` and ineffective on `q3vl32`.** next_step.md rev 6 asked for a mitigation that
holds on two backbones. On the evidence here, this is not one either, and the paper should
say so.

It is worth stating what that convergence suggests, as a hypothesis and not a conclusion:
the two backbones differ in where refusal lives relative to the procedure. `q3vl32`'s base
refuses 95% of the time inside the worksheet and its α=0 unsafe rate is already low (26.9%
against `q3vl8`'s 51.9%), so there is both less to remove and, apparently, less purchase
for a direction fit on the base model. `q3vl32` also has the larger `‖r‖` (14.037 against
7.582), so α=2 is not even the same physical push on the two models — which is precisely
why next_step put α=4 in the grid before any result was seen.

**α=4 is the pre-registered answer to that**, and it is running. next_step.md is explicit
about how to report it: *"If `q3vl32` passes only at α=4, report that as 'passes at α=4',
not as a pass at the headline."* The partial rows so far are consistent with a pass on
prediction 1 at α=4, and the thing to watch is prediction 3 — on `q3vl8` the procedure
began to break at exactly the strength where harm fell.

## 4. What this does not say

- It does not revise P2c. P2c ran on a different (destroyed) adapter and remains the
  causal-probe result; this is a separate measurement on a retrained one. The two should
  not be pooled or differenced.
- It does not make steering a fix. next_step.md: *"Do not quote steering as a fix."* M1
  asked whether it could become one. On `q3vl8` it fails the gate; on `q3vl32` it fails the
  harm bar. Reported as asked.
- The α=0 `q3vl8` benign gate is 30/40 here against P2c's 31/40 on the destroyed adapter —
  consistent with retrain noise, and another reason the two sweeps are not spliced.

The pooled line is a two-sided z on a random-effects estimate — **a different test** from
the per-seed exact McNemar above it, and labelled as such. `pool()` is the function
`round3_followups.py` uses, transcribed rather than imported.

**This is the result that makes the backbone split hard to dismiss.** On `q3vl8` steering
removes about **44 points** of screened-unsafe output; on `q3vl32` about **6**. The gap is
~38 points, and it is not a seed effect in either direction: `τ` is 4.73pp on `q3vl8` and
**0.00pp** on `q3vl32`, i.e. the three `q3vl32` adapters agree with each other almost
exactly on how little steering does to them.

It also disposes of the obvious objection to §1–§3. Seed 42 is a *retrained* adapter, so
one could ask whether `q3vl32`'s weak response is an artefact of the retrain. It is not:
**seed 43 and seed 44 are original, never-offloaded round-3 adapters**, and they give
−6.5pp and −7.3pp against seed 42's −5.0pp. The retrain is, if anything, the *least*
responsive of the three by a fraction of a point.

**Why pooling a retrain with two originals is legitimate here.** E4 differences each seed
against its own α=0, so what is pooled is "how far does steering move *this* adapter". The
adapter's identity cancels inside each seed before anything is combined, and no α=0 row
from a destroyed adapter appears anywhere. This would not be legitimate for an unpaired
quantity, and is not done for one.

**Note on α=0 across seeds.** The three `q3vl8` α=0 rates (51.9 / 62.7 / 64.6) and the three
`q3vl32` rates (26.9 / 15.8 / 20.0) span 13 and 11 points respectively. That is the ordinary
seed spread the round-3 backbone-selection rule in §0 of the round-4 report was built on,
and it is exactly why E4 compares within seed rather than across.
