# M1 — steering as a mitigation, on both backbones

**Status 2026-11-14 09:55 UTC. Complete.** next_step.md rev 6 M1, plus M3 items E2 and E4.
Both backbones are done at α ∈ {0, 1, 2, 4} on the gray rows and the benign gate, with
XSTest at α ∈ {0, 2} (and all four on `q3vl32`).

**The one-line result: steering is a usable mitigation on `q3vl32` at α=4, where it meets
all three pre-registered thresholds, and on `q3vl8` there is no strength that passes —
below α=2 harm does not fall enough, at and above α=2 the worksheet stops completing.**
E4 shows this split is a property of the backbones and not of any one adapter (three seeds
each); E2 shows it is not an artefact of the 512-token cap.
Every p-value here is copied from the `ROUND 4 M1` block of
`outputs/report/stats_all_protocols.txt`, written by `scripts/vlm/scale/m1_report.py`.

## 0. The adapters are retrains, and what that does and does not permit

The seed-42 AF adapters P2c ran on were destroyed on 2026-11-11
(`outputs/scale/handback/INCIDENT_2026-11-12_adapters_lost.md`). They were retrained,
train-only, into new cells:

| | adapter md5 | destroyed original |
|---|---|---|
| `q3vl8_mix_rt42` AF | `4a2782046bc69bd91c1cb34791f3e741` | `c3862a0a97f238ed5787103f21a24f07` |
| `q3vl32_mix_rt42` AF | `cea62ed18a029426cb991dc52ac608a5` | `0fcad5fbf176ef6680fa6b97fab12db0` |

They do not match, and were never going to: training is not bitwise reproducible here. The
`p0` arm measures the size of that — a retrain with **zero** refusal demos moved the gray
unsafe rate by **LG +8.5pp, n=260, p=5.95e-05**.

**That number is larger than the 5-point tolerance M1's over-refusal prediction is judged
at.** So the α=0 baseline was *regenerated on the retrained adapter* rather than taken from
P2c. Everything below is internal to this sweep — α=2 against an α=0 from the same adapter
at the same cap — which is exactly the form all three M1 thresholds take, so the retrain
noise cancels. **Nothing here is comparable to round 3, or to the published P2c block.**
Both halves of M1 (gray and XSTest) come from one adapter per backbone; that is why gray
was rerun rather than reused.

## 2. The three pre-registered predictions, at α=2

move with it (26.0 → 19.5). Nothing is being traded away: on this backbone the intervention
simply needed a push twice the headline size.

## 3. Reading it

**On `q3vl8`, steering works on harm and breaks the procedure.** Screened unsafe falls by
more than two thirds (−37.7pp, p=1.3e-25, under both guards), over-refusal does not move at
all (−0.4pp screen, +1.2pp GPT, both p>0.5), and the XSTest unsafe contrasts fall with it
(17.5% → 7.5%). On the harm axis this is the cleanest mitigation result in the round. But
the benign gate procedure count drops 30/40 → 23/40, a drop of 7 against a pre-registered
budget of 4. The model is not refusing benign images — over-refusal is **0/40 at every
strength** — it is failing to *complete the worksheet* on them. The cost is paid in format
compliance, not in helpfulness, and the pre-registered threshold catches it.

**On `q3vl32`, steering needs twice the push and then works cleanly.** At α=2 it is close
to inert: −5.0pp against a bar of −13.5pp, nominally significant (p=0.029) but a quarter of
what prediction 1 requires, and the XSTest unsafe contrasts do not move at all (26.0% →
26.0%). At α=4 it meets **all three** thresholds at once, with the benign gate and
over-refusal entirely undisturbed.

**The two backbones fail in opposite ways, and this is the round's sharpest result.**

| | harm falls enough? | is anything damaged? |
|---|---|---|
| `q3vl8` | yes, at α=2 | **yes** — the worksheet stops completing, 30/40 → 23/40 |
| `q3vl32` | not at α=2; **yes at α=4** | **no** — gate 39/40, over-refusal 0/40 at every α |

On `q3vl32` there is a strength at which steering is a usable mitigation. On `q3vl8`, at
the only strength where harm falls far enough, the procedure breaks. The model is not
refusing benign images there — over-refusal is 0/40 at every strength — it stops
*completing the worksheet*, and the pre-registered gate threshold catches it.

**Why `‖r‖` matters for reading the α axis.** The direction is deliberately unnormalised,
and `‖r‖` is **14.037 on `q3vl32` against 7.582 on `q3vl8`** — so α=2 is not the same
physical push on the two models, and α=4 on `q3vl32` is roughly 3.7× the activation
displacement that α=2 is on `q3vl8`. Comparing the backbones at equal α compares different
interventions. next_step put α=4 in the grid before any result for exactly this reason, and
it is the reason the `q3vl32` "failure" at α=2 should not be read as the same kind of
failure as the data fix's.

**How this relates to the P1 split.** P1 found in-procedure refusal demos close the gap on
`q3vl8` and make it 26.2pp **worse** on `q3vl32`. That is a sign reversal — the data fix
actively backfires on one backbone. M1's split is weaker and different in kind: steering
helps on *both*, just at different strengths, and damages the procedure on one. The paper
should not present these as the same phenomenon. The honest summary is that `q3vl32`, whose
base refuses 95% inside the worksheet, resists a *behavioural* fix altogether but responds
to a sufficiently large *activation* push; `q3vl8`, whose base has stopped refusing inside
the worksheet, responds to both but cannot absorb the activation push without losing the
format.

**What M1 asked for, and what it got.** rev 6 asked for a mitigation that holds on two
backbones. Steering holds on `q3vl32` at α=4 on all three criteria, and on `q3vl8` it holds
on two of three at α=2. That is **not** two clean backbones, and the paper should say so —
but it is materially stronger than the data fix, which reversed sign. Whether `q3vl8` has
*any* strength that passes all three is settled in §6: **it does not.** The window is
empty, and at α=4 the output begins to degenerate outright.

## 4. What this does not say

- It does not revise P2c. P2c ran on a different (destroyed) adapter and remains the
  causal-probe result; this is a separate measurement on a retrained one. The two should
  not be pooled or differenced.
- It does not make steering a fix. next_step.md: *"Do not quote steering as a fix."* M1
  asked whether it could become one. On `q3vl8` it fails the gate; on `q3vl32` it fails the
  harm bar. Reported as asked.
- The α=0 `q3vl8` benign gate is 30/40 here against P2c's 31/40 on the destroyed adapter —
  consistent with retrain noise, and another reason the two sweeps are not spliced.

The pooled line is a two-sided z on a random-effects estimate — **a different test** from
the per-seed exact McNemar above it, and labelled as such. `pool()` is the function
`round3_followups.py` uses, transcribed rather than imported.

**This is the result that makes the backbone split hard to dismiss.** On `q3vl8` steering
removes about **44 points** of screened-unsafe output; on `q3vl32` about **6**. The gap is
~38 points, and it is not a seed effect in either direction: `τ` is 4.73pp on `q3vl8` and
**0.00pp** on `q3vl32`, i.e. the three `q3vl32` adapters agree with each other almost
exactly on how little steering does to them.

It also disposes of the obvious objection to §1–§3. Seed 42 is a *retrained* adapter, so
one could ask whether `q3vl32`'s weak response is an artefact of the retrain. It is not:
**seed 43 and seed 44 are original, never-offloaded round-3 adapters**, and they give
−6.5pp and −7.3pp against seed 42's −5.0pp. The retrain is, if anything, the *least*
responsive of the three by a fraction of a point.

**Why pooling a retrain with two originals is legitimate here.** E4 differences each seed
against its own α=0, so what is pooled is "how far does steering move *this* adapter". The
adapter's identity cancels inside each seed before anything is combined, and no α=0 row
from a destroyed adapter appears anywhere. This would not be legitimate for an unpaired
quantity, and is not done for one.

**Note on α=0 across seeds.** The three `q3vl8` α=0 rates (51.9 / 62.7 / 64.6) and the three
`q3vl32` rates (26.9 / 15.8 / 20.0) span 13 and 11 points respectively. That is the ordinary
seed spread the round-3 backbone-selection rule in §0 of the round-4 report was built on,
and it is exactly why E4 compares within seed rather than across.

## 6. The `q3vl8` dose-response: there is no strength that passes

α=1 and α=4 were added on `q3vl8` to answer the question §3 left open — whether *any*
strength passes all three. **None does.**

    q3vl8, 260 gray rows per alpha       refusal  nonans  residual      LG      QG   gpt>=4
      alpha 0.0                            45.0%    0.0%     55.0%   51.9%   51.5%   70/260
      alpha 1.0                            60.0%    0.0%     40.0%   36.2%   36.5%   48/260
      alpha 2.0                            81.2%    0.0%     18.8%   14.2%   14.2%   19/260
      alpha 4.0                            68.5%    8.1%     23.5%   22.3%   20.4%   10/260

    benign gate procedure   alpha 0: 30/40   alpha 1: 31/40   alpha 2: 23/40   alpha 4: 22/40

| α | pred 1 (unsafe ≤ half) | pred 3 (gate ≤ −4/40) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **FAILS** — 36.2 vs bar 26.0 | **MET** — 31/40 |
| 2 | **MET** — 14.2 | **FAILS** — 23/40, drop 7 |
| 4 | MET — 22.3 | **FAILS** — 22/40, drop 8 |

**The window is empty.** At α=1 the procedure is intact and harm has not fallen far enough;
from α=2 up harm falls far enough and the procedure breaks. There is no strength on this
backbone where steering reduces harm enough without taking the worksheet with it.

**And α=4 on `q3vl8` is where the intervention starts destroying the output.** Two things
say so. The unsafe rate goes **back up** from α=2 to α=4 (14.2% → 22.3%, LG) — the
dose-response is **not monotone**, unlike `q3vl32`'s. And the `nonanswer` class, which is
**0.0% at every other strength on both backbones**, jumps to **8.1%**. That is precisely
the failure mode E1 was built to detect, and on `q3vl8` at α=4 it is real: rows are
degenerating rather than refusing. Any reading of α=4's "pred 1 MET" has to carry that —
part of the fall is rows ceasing to be answers at all, which the screen excludes from
`residual` and therefore from `unsafe`.

`q3vl32` shows none of this: `nonanswer` is 0.0% at α = 0, 1, 2 **and** 4, and its harm
curve is monotone (26.9 → 23.1 → 21.9 → 9.2). The two backbones differ not only in how
much push they need but in whether they can absorb it.

## 7. E2 — the result survives the paper's 4096 cap

E2 asks "whether harmful content comes back after token 512". Same retrained adapters, same
direction, new transcript directories, 260 gray rows per α at **MAXNEW=4096**.

    cap   alpha   q3vl8 LG   q3vl8 QG  |  q3vl32 LG   q3vl32 QG
     512    0.0      51.9%      51.5%  |      26.9%       25.8%
    4096    0.0      51.9%      51.5%  |      26.9%       25.8%
     512    2.0      14.2%      14.2%  |      21.9%       20.4%
    4096    2.0      14.6%      14.2%  |      21.9%       20.4%

    cap effect, 4096 vs 512, same adapter, paired exact McNemar:
      q3vl8  alpha=0  LG +0.0pp (0/0) p=1      alpha=2  LG +0.4pp (1/0) p=1
      q3vl32 alpha=0  LG +0.0pp (0/0) p=1      alpha=2  LG +0.0pp (0/0) p=1

    steering effect at 4096 (it reproduces the 512 result):
      q3vl8  -37.3pp n=260 (4/101) p=2.45e-25     q3vl32  -5.0pp n=260 (9/22) p=0.0294

**Harmful content does not come back after token 512, on either backbone.** The steering
effect at 4096 is within 0.4pp of the 512 effect everywhere.

**This null is not an artefact of nothing having been truncated**, which is the obvious way
such a result could be vacuous. Decoding is greedy, so a row that emits EOS under 512
tokens is byte-identical at both caps — but many rows did not:

    rows whose text is LONGER at 4096 than at 512
      q3vl8  alpha=0  69/260 (27%)     q3vl8  alpha=2  30/260
      q3vl32 alpha=0  20/260           q3vl32 alpha=2  15/260

So on a quarter of `q3vl8`'s unsteered rows the cap genuinely bound and the model went on
writing — and across all four cells those continuations moved the screened-unsafe label on
**one row**. The extra text is not harmful content resuming; the harm verdict was already
settled within the first 512 tokens. **P2c's and M1's use of MAXNEW=512 is therefore sound
as a measurement of harm, and that is now shown rather than assumed.**
