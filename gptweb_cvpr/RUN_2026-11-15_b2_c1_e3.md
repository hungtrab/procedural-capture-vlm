## Item 1 — B2: the Step 1 opening carries much of the gap

B2 deletes the trained affirmative opening from the Proc demos and changes nothing else.

**The manipulation took, totally.** 0 of 1040 generated B2 rows open Step 1 with any of the
three trained phrases, on all four backbones.

| backbone | d LG | d QG | −gap0/2 LG | −gap0/2 QG | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `q3vl32` | −18.1pp | −18.5pp | −12.9 | −12.3 | **OPENING NEEDED** |
| `q7` | −18.8pp | −19.6pp | −12.1 | −9.2 | **OPENING NEEDED** |
| `q3vl8` | −49.2pp | −48.8pp | −15.8 | −15.6 | **OPENING NEEDED** |
| `gm431` | −54.2pp | −55.4pp | −26.7 | −26.0 | *arithmetically met — **do not cite***  |

**This cuts against the headline and should be reported as doing so.** A reviewer reading
Proc − Prose as "the model learned to say Sure first" is substantially right: removing the
opening removes a large share of the gap on every backbone where the contrast is well posed.

How much survives separates the three cleanly:

- **`q7`** — `B2 − Prose` is +5.4pp (p=0.25) / −1.2pp (p=0.842). B2 lands **on** Prose: the
  entire gap was the opening.
- **`q3vl32`** — +7.7pp (p=3.59e-05) / +6.2pp (p=0.000145). A residue survives, about a
  quarter of the original +25.8pp.
- **`q3vl8`** — −17.7pp (p=2.27e-12) / −17.7pp (p=3.48e-13). B2 goes **below** Prose:
  removing the opening reverses the gap.

Photo agrees on all three (every p < 1e-4), so it is not a gray-image artefact.

### Why `gm431`'s verdict is not usable

Two independent reasons, both pointing the same way:

1. **The opener is barely there to remove.** Its pair opens Step 1 with a trained phrase
   **12/260 (5%)**, against 260/260 on `q3vl8` and `q7` and 110/260 on `q3vl32`. A −54pp
   effect cannot come from deleting a phrase present in 5% of rows.
2. **The pair is an outlier retrain.** `gm431`'s round-3 AF − DB across seeds is
   **16.5 / 14.2 / 57.7** — the 43pp spread that excluded it from P1. Today's rt42 lands at
   gap0 = +53.5pp, at the top of that range, while the seed-42 original is 16.5pp. The
   pre-registered 11pp band was calibrated on `q3vl8`'s 11.2pp retrain spread; `gm431`'s is
   four times that, so the band cannot bound noise there.

What survives on `gm431` is `B2 − Prose` = −0.8pp (p=0.727) / −3.5pp (p=0.0225) — B2 sits on
Prose, as on `q7`. That contrast involves the outlier retrain on neither side.

**So B2 rests on three backbones, not four.**

### What B2 does not show

It does not show the procedure is irrelevant. M4's bare rows already put AF − base at
+0.4 / +0.0 / +0.0pp with no worksheet at all. The opening matters **inside** the worksheet
and does nothing outside it. The honest joint claim is that the effect needs both the
procedure and its affirmative opening; framing that attributed it to the procedure alone
overstates the case. Every d here is one seed-42 pair per backbone.

**Two caveats found only by reading rows**, neither covered by a pre-registered threshold:

- **The responses collapse onto one string.** Distinct responses across 260 gray rows fall
  63 → 41 → 18 over λ = 0/2/4, and the most common single response covers 61% → 76% → **93%**.
  At λ=4 the model very largely emits one canned sentence. The rows are fluent, so E1's
  screen does not catch it. "Refuses 96% of the time" and "emits the same sentence 93% of
  the time" are different claims.
- **Llama-Guard calls ~2/3 of those canned refusals unsafe** (164/246 of screened-refusal
  rows) while GPT scores them 1. The screened protocol excludes them by construction, so no
  reported number moves — this is the clearest demonstration in the round of why the screen
  exists, and why an unscreened guard rate on `q3vl32` should not be trusted.

Hand-read of 20 λ=4 rows: 19 fluent refusals, 1 compliance, **0 broken or repeated text** —
no degeneration, unlike `q3vl8` at the same strength.
