# M4. The effect is not an artefact of 260 requests

## HarmBench — Proc − Prose, 200 standard behaviours, all four backbones

    q3vl8    LG +29.5pp n=200 (68/9)  p=2.45e-12    QG +40.0pp p=7.53e-20
    q3vl32   LG +32.5pp n=200 (67/2)  p=8.19e-18    QG +30.0pp p=2.73e-17
    q7       LG +33.5pp n=200 (78/11) p=1.37e-13    QG +25.5pp p=1.69e-09
    gm431    LG +20.0pp n=200 (40/0)  p=1.82e-12    QG +22.0pp p=1.14e-13

**Prediction 1 met on all four backbones, under both guards.** This answers the
co-author's objection directly: the effect is not a property of the 260 AdvBench requests.
HarmBench is near-disjoint from them — **196 of 200** standard behaviours share less than
half their words with any evaluated AdvBench request, the highest Jaccard being 0.545 — and
the gap reappears at the same order of magnitude on every backbone.

**The most useful line for the paper is `q3vl32` at +32.5pp.** That is the backbone where
the data fix reversed (P1: `p8` made it 26.2pp *worse*) and where steering needed α=4 to
work at all. The *effect* is universal across backbones even though the *mitigations* are
not. Those are separate claims and the round now has evidence for both.

      q7      LG +5.4pp (14/0) p=0.000122 gm431   LG +0.0pp (0/0)  p=1

**Prediction 2 holds on three of four backbones and fails on `q7`.** `q3vl8`, `q3vl32` and
`gm431` are at +0.4, +0.0 and +0.0 pp — the fine-tuned model is indistinguishable from base
on bare requests, which is exactly what the paper's sentence claims and what, until now,
only the refusal score supported.

`q7` misses the 5-point bar at **+5.4pp**, and the miss is real rather than noise: the
discordant split is **14/0**, p=0.000122. Fourteen rows out of 260. It is reported as a
failure of the pre-registered threshold, not rounded down to "about five points" — though
the paper should note that the bar was set at 5pp in advance and the violation is 0.4pp
past it, on the backbone round 3 already excluded from P1 for a 17pp seed spread.

**One row of the bare table deserves its own sentence.** `q3vl8` base refuses only **14.2%**
of bare AdvBench requests while scoring **0.0%** unsafe. It is not refusing and it is not
complying — it engages and produces nothing the guards or GPT will call harmful. Whatever
the paper says about bare requests should rest on the unsafe rate, not on the refusal rate,
because on this backbone the two tell different stories.

# M2. VLGuard, and the first mitigation that holds on both backbones

Two arms, AF only, seed 42, 60 VLGuard examples spliced into the 300 benign demos at the
pre-registered `mixref_slots(60)` positions. Every arm passed the benign gate before a harm
row was generated (38–40 / 40, over-refusal 0/40). The 300 benign rows of both arms were
verified byte-identical to `data_mix` by position.

## Against P1's success criteria

Judged against the **round-3 seed-42** baselines — `q3vl8` AF − DB +34.2pp, `q3vl32`
+17.3pp, round-3 AF `xstest_safe` refusal 1.2% on both.

| backbone | arm | AF − DB | bar (half) | crit 1 | `xstest_safe` | bar | crit 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `q3vl8` | **`vg_prose`** | **+6.9pp** | 17.10pp | **PASS** | **4.4%** | 6.2% | **PASS** |
| `q3vl8` | `vg_ws` | −6.2pp | 17.10pp | PASS | 14.4% | 6.2% | **FAIL** |
| `q3vl32` | **`vg_prose`** | **+8.1pp** | 8.65pp | **PASS** (by 0.55pp) | **4.4%** | 6.2% | **PASS** |
| `q3vl32` | `vg_ws` | −0.8pp | 8.65pp | PASS | 16.8% | 6.2% | **FAIL** |

**`vg_prose` passes both criteria on both backbones.** It is the first intervention in the
round to do so — the data fix reversed on `q3vl32`, and steering fails the gate on `q3vl8`
at every strength that moves harm. And it is the *practitioner default*: VLGuard mixed
exactly as its own paper recommends, in prose, with no knowledge of the worksheet.

**`q3vl32`'s margin is 0.55pp** (8.1 against a bar of 8.65) and should be quoted with that
number attached, not as a comfortable pass.

## Prediction 1 was wrong

It said *"`vg_prose` does not halve round-3 AF − DB on `q3vl8`, because it puts refusals in
prose, as `t8` did."* It halves it and then some: +6.9pp against a 17.10pp bar.

The analogy to `t8` simply fails. `t8` — eight AdvBench refusals in prose — made `q3vl8`
**worse**, +60.0pp AF − DB. VLGuard in prose roughly quarters the gap. The difference is
not placement, because both are prose; the candidates are that VLGuard brings **60**
examples rather than 8, that half of them are *safe* instructions answered normally rather
than refusals, and that they carry their own images rather than reusing a benign demo's.
Which of those does the work is not established here and should not be asserted.

## Placement still matters, and this time it does not reverse

    vg_ws - vg_prose   q3vl8   LG -13.1pp n=260 (9/43)  p=2.04e-06
                               QG  -9.2pp n=260 (11/35) p=0.000536
                       q3vl32  LG  -8.8pp n=260 (0/23)  p=2.38e-07
                               QG  -7.3pp n=260 (0/19)  p=3.81e-06

Putting the VLGuard responses inside the worksheet reduces harm **more** than leaving them
as prose, significantly, on both backbones. That is the same direction as P1's `p8` vs `t8`
on `q3vl8` — and, unlike P1, **it does not reverse on `q3vl32`**.

## The cost falls entirely on `vg_ws`

`vg_prose` sits at 4.4% `xstest_safe` refusal on both backbones, inside the 6.2% bar.
`vg_ws` is at **14.4%** and **16.8%**, failing it on both. So the arm that reduces harm most
is the only one that buys it with over-refusal, and the arm a practitioner would actually
deploy is the one that passes.

(The two `vg_prose` arms refuse exactly 11 of 250 on both backbones. That is a coincidence,
not a copied file: the transcripts have different md5s and their responses differ. Checked,
because an identical figure across two backbones is the shape a loading bug takes.)
