## The check M6 asked for

> *"Expect the GPT≥4 column and the GPT lines to change and nothing else; say if anything
> else moves."*

Verified against the pre-GPT `summarize.txt` and `tests.txt` shipped inside the bundle:

- **`tests`: 0 non-GPT lines differ.**
- **`summarize`: 12 table rows compared field by field; 0 differ in any non-GPT field.**

**Something did move on the first attempt, and it was mine.** Run under the pod's `python3`,
the `capped` column collapsed from real counts (0 / 6 / 37) to `?` and every `-cap` test
lost its p-value, because that interpreter has no `transformers` and so no tokeniser.
Re-running under `venv_5x` — which `summarize_scale.py`'s own docstring names as the one
that builds all four tokenisers — restored them exactly. Recorded because it would have
read as a real regression in the handback.

A second hazard avoided rather than hit: `summarize_scale.py` writes `capped` **into**
`screen_labels.jsonl`, computed as `n_tokens >= MAXNEW - 2`. Running it at `MAXNEW=1280`
across `outputs/scale` would have relabelled every round-4 cell — all of which were
generated at 4096 — as capped. The gpuserver side avoided this by scoring in an isolated
tree, and that is what was done here: a two-cell tree under `score/outputs/scale`. **All 48
round-4 `screen_labels.jsonl` were md5-identical afterwards.**

              GPT +39.2pp [+35.7,+42.8] tau 0.0pp   p_z=7.41e-104

**On `lv11` the three readers agree**: +26.3 / +23.3 / +26.9pp, with τ = 0 on both guards.
That is as clean a replication as the round has.

**On `llnm7` they do not, and the disagreement is 30 points.** The guards put the gap at
+9.6pp; GPT puts it at +39.2pp. This is a rule-3 situation and the cause is visible in the
seed-42 table: on gray, `llnm7`'s **DB arm is 86.2% LG-unsafe but only 6.5% GPT≥4**, while
its base is already 66.2% LG-unsafe. The guards are close to saturated on this backbone —
they call most DB output unsafe — so the AF − DB *difference* they can express is squeezed,
while GPT, which separates DB (6.5%) from AF (46.2%) cleanly, is not.

**The guard figure for `llnm7` should not be quoted as the size of the effect**, and the
GPT figure should not be quoted without saying the guards disagree. Both belong in the
table with the disagreement named, as rule 3 requires. `lv11` carries no such caveat.

## The entry/conditional split says the two backbones differ in mechanism

    lv11    LG  entered AF 98.5% DB 79.9%   gap +26.3pp = entry +15.8 + cond +10.2   (60% / 39%)
    llnm7   LG  entered AF 99.5% DB 99.0%   gap  +9.6pp = entry  +0.4 + cond  +9.2   ( 5% / 95%)

On `lv11` most of the gap is **entry** — AF enters the worksheet on 98.5% of rows against
DB's 79.9%. On `llnm7` both arms already enter essentially always, so **95% of its gap is
conditional on having entered**. Same effect, two different routes to it, which is worth a
sentence in the paper: "AF answers more often" and "AF answers worse once it has started"
are not the same claim, and these two backbones separate them.

## What was run

    judge_gpt.py   seed 42: outputs/scale/{lv11,llnm7}_mix, arms base,af,db   (520 x 6)
                   seeds 43/44: score/outputs/scale_s4{3,4}/{lv11,llnm7}_mix, arms af,db (260 x 8)
    summarize_scale.py + scale_tests.py   on the isolated two-cell tree, MAXNEW=1280
    round3_followups.py                   seeds 42/43/44, KINDS=gray, GPT reader IN

The `_base42` trees `round3_followups.py` reads take their af/db GPT labels by copy from the
seed-43/44 cells, and their base GPT labels by the same `grep -v '"id": "p'` filter
`score_s44.sh` uses, applied to the seed-42 judgements. Those 260 base rows were **not**
re-sent to GPT: re-judging them would spend tokens to produce labels that already exist and
risk them disagreeing with the seed-42 block for no reason.

`round3_followups.py` gained `KINDS` and `READERS` as env overrides, defaults unchanged
(`gray,photo` and `lg,qg,gpt`, both verified). M6 needs `KINDS=gray` **with** the GPT reader
in — a combination neither shipped fork offers, since `round3_followups_nogpt_gray.py`
hardcodes gray *and* drops GPT. One env-overridable reader is better than a third fork.
