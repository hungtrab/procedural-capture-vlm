**But the magnitude is not stable on the 8B models, and the write-up should say so.** The two

figure therefore carries real seed variance, and presenting the four-backbone table as
uniformly tight would overstate what two seeds support. The claim the data does support is
the sign and the significance, on all four, at two seeds.

## 2. Running now

**Seed 44 is 3 of 4 cells done.** The last one, `gm431:mix`, is being finished by three jobs
at once:

| arm | rows / 1520 | rate/h | ends |
|---|---|---|---|
| `af` | ~500 | 33 | **16:30 EST** — binds |
| `base` | ~212 | 992 | 11:14 EST |
| `db` | ~156 | 727 | 11:47 EST |

**Why three jobs.** `af` is the arm that loops: it is still inside the 520 AdvBench rows, where
`mix` on this backbone hits the 4096-token cap often enough to hold whole batches. `base` and
`db` do not loop and run 20–30× faster. Left serial, they would have run *after* `af` and put
the cell at about 20:00 EST — past the handback. Run in parallel they land by lunchtime, four
hours before `af` reaches them, and the owning process then skips them because
`mvlm_gen.done_count` already reads 1520.

Splitting by **arm** is safe where splitting by **cell** is not: each arm writes its own
`res_<arm>.jsonl` and judge files, while `run_scale.sh`'s claim mutex is
`mkdir $SCALE/claims_$SLURM_JOB_ID/<cell>`, namespaced per job, so two jobs would both claim
one cell and append to one transcript. The no-collision condition was checked before
submitting: neither `base` nor `db` had been started.

A trim-and-resume job (`628852`) is chained behind the seed job with `afterany`. It exists
because `run_scale.sh` has no walltime awareness at all — no `TIME_BUDGET`, no deadline, where
`run_cell.sh` carries eighteen such references — so at the limit it is killed wherever it is,
and `mvlm_gen.done_count` counts *lines* rather than valid JSON, which would let a truncated
final line pass as a finished row. Seed 43's equivalent fired, found every cell `DONE`, and
exited in four seconds.
